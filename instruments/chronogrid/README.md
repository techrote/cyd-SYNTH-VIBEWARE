# CHRONOGRID M3 — spatial polymetric percussion sequencer

Original standalone four-track instrument. Sample-clock integer phase accumulators share a BPM but each has independent length and subdivision: kick 16 steps / 4 per beat; snare 12 / 4; hat 10 / 3; tone 7 / 5. Their combined cycle moves through different rhythmic alignments. Fixed four monophonic voices, deterministic noise, swept triangle kick, noise/triangle snare, differentiated-noise hat, pitched triangle texture. No sample library, external MIDI, wireless, pressure or multitouch required.

## Play
- Top six numbered buttons: Crossings (108 BPM), Sparse (78), Orbit (96), Broken (132), Dense (150), Counterlight (112). Recall replaces the volatile pattern and tempo, preserving current clock phase and mutes.
- Four lanes: red kick, cyan snare, yellow hat, purple tone. Bright cells are enabled; white borders show actual audio-clock playheads. Tap a cell to toggle; sweep horizontally to paint/erase using the initial cell's opposite state, filling skipped cells. A gesture stays in its original lane.
- Tap the left half of the lane header to mute/unmute; tap the upper-right + to toggle accent editing for that lane; tap the lower-right number to cycle that lane's clock subdivision. Paint or erase accents with the same horizontal gesture. Gold marks show accents on enabled steps; dark gold marks retain accents on disabled steps without triggering them. Return to note editing with another + tap. Scene recall clears accents and edit modes. Bottom: transport, reset to step zero, -4 BPM, +4 BPM, panic. Tempo 50–180 BPM; the small number is under minus. Tempo changes preserve fractional phase. Stop/start resets phase and deterministic noise. Reset button restarts at step zero if playing; it also explicitly recovers from a stale heartbeat. Panic stops immediately in generated PCM, and held input must be released before new input. Focus/capture loss and Escape stop on Windows.
- Desktop: chronogrid.exe, mouse single-contact, system volume initially low. Real GDI and waveOut, four 128-sample blocks at 24 kHz. Already queued audio cannot be recalled by software panic alone. --smoke exercises actual graphical window, scripted core interaction, nonzero generated audio and silence after panic; it does not open an audio endpoint.
- CYD candidate: classic ESP32-2432S028R, GPIO26 internal DAC via I2S, candidate ILI9341/XPT2046 mapping, 4-row transfer service, no full framebuffer/PSRAM requirement. Serial 'a' explicitly arms, 'm'/BOOT mute; touch transport remains stopped until user starts. Board-specific touch calibration and audio startup NOT RUN physically. No upload/flash instructions or installable images distributed.

## Engineering limits
Single-writer atomic 5-word control payload and generation, one bounded read attempt (retain previous state on collision), heartbeat stops the engine after nominal 250ms of generated audio; no implicit restart from fresh heartbeat. No audio-owner locks, allocation, display or network. Render uses caller-owned RGB565 line storage and actual audio step snapshot, not synthetic timer animation. Phase clock is sample-counted; edits and transport are block-boundary control snapshots, not a sample-timestamped event queue; very rapid intervening states may coalesce. Phase reset has 8-bit epoch; no persisted/wire format, no save/load, no cross-track rules or persisted patterns yet. Hard mute/reset may click, and percussion is intentionally non-bandlimited noise/triangle; no alias or acoustic measurements. Initial native tests are author evidence; target/Windows author CI and independent/physical gates outstanding at publication. Draft/HOLD.

## M3 — selectable live clock subdivisions
Each lane has a lower-right numeric button, cycling 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 and 8 steps per beat independently; the upper-right + retains accent mode. A held tap cycles only once. Scene recall restores the original 4/4/3/5 subdivisions alongside scene tempo and pattern; live ratio changes and scene recall preserve the fractional sample-clock phase and do not retrigger or reset voices. A bounded atomic payload word carries four validated ratios; invalid values normalize to 4. Increment is calculated once per block per lane, not per sample.
